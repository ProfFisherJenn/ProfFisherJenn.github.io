Before you start

What size is a pixel?

It depends on the resolution. Resolution, measured in pixels per inch (ppi), is how many times an inch is divided up. At 5 ppi each pixel is 1/5 of an inch wide. At 25 ppi each pixel is only 1/25 of an inch.

One inch, divided four ways

Pixels are always square. When there are only a few per inch, those square edges show, and curves look blocky and pixelated. With more pixels per inch, the squares get small enough to blend together, and the same curve looks round and smooth.

How this works

  1. Do the math. Solve the resolution equation to unlock each canvas.
  2. Paint the smiley. Click and drag to fill pixels. Hold Alt (Option on Mac) and drag to erase. Pick a bigger brush size when you need it.
  3. Paint at least 25%. Once a quarter of the smiley's pixels are yours, click Fill in the answer to complete the level.
  4. Repeat at 5, 10, 20 and 25 ppi. The canvas is 5 inches every time. Only the number of pixels changes.
  5. Compare and reflect. Look at all four smileys side by side, write a short reflection and answer two quick questions.
  6. Continue to the Image Size Explorer. Practice Photoshop's Image Size settings, then answer a few more questions.
  7. Download your certificate. At the end of the Image Size Explorer, download your certificate (PNG) and upload it to the Pixel Smiley Lab assignment in Canvas.
Real-world resolutions: 72–96 ppi for screens · 200 ppi for print · 300 ppi for high-quality and glossy print. This lab uses very low resolutions (5–25 ppi) only so each pixel is big enough to see and paint.
